Interpretation

A positive reading indicates that the weighted indicators, after direction adjustment, are above their historical baselines on balance.

The UKPI is a research indicator. It is not a percentage, recession probability, official statistic or forecast.

Methodology

1. Historical standardisation

Each indicator is compared with its own preceding five-year historical window. The observation being scored is excluded from the baseline.

z = (observation − historical mean) ÷ population standard deviation

A score of +1 means one standard deviation above the historical mean; −1 means one below. Insufficient history or a zero standard deviation can prevent a component from being scored.

2. Direction adjustment

Indicator directions are adjusted so higher component scores are intended to represent greater economic stress. For example, higher unemployment and volatility increase stress, while stronger growth and confidence reduce it.

3. Composite indicators

Job security combines redundancy and vacancy-related measures. Confidence and uncertainty combines consumer confidence, the Lloyds Business Barometer and UK economic policy uncertainty.

4. Weights and aggregation

Component scores are combined using configured weights. When components are unavailable, weights are renormalised across those that can be scored. The effective weights shown in the table sum to 100%.

UKPI = weighted average of component z-scores × 25

The factor of 25 scales the result for presentation. The index is not a percentage or probability, and its magnitude does not directly represent the probability of a particular economic outcome.

5. Reporting periods and data lags

The intended live reference month is the last completed calendar month. Some source series are released quarterly or with delays, so an observation may describe an earlier period. Publication lags can therefore leave the latest reading provisional.

6. Limitations

The index measures conditions relative to recent historical baselines, not absolute economic health. Results can be affected by data revisions, missing observations, overlapping indicators, and the use of proxy data for some components.

Data sources

  • Office for National Statistics: inflation, GDP, public debt, labour-market data and earnings.
  • Bank of England: Bank Rate and exchange-rate data.
  • FTSE volatility input: ISF.LON ETF price proxy, not a direct FTSE 100 index series.
  • Federal Reserve Economic Data: UK Economic Policy Uncertainty.
  • GfK and Lloyds Banking Group: consumer confidence and business sentiment.
